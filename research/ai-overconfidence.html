AI-Fueled Overconfidence: AI Assistance, Time Pressure, and Risk-Taking in Tournaments

How does AI-assisted confidence shape the competitions people choose to enter?

Status
Experimental design in progress
Period
2026–present
Role
First author
Advisor
Prof. Changjun Lee

Overview

This project asks how AI assistance shapes the way people judge their own abilities and choose competitive opportunities. It focuses on overplacement—an overly favorable assessment of one’s standing relative to others—and its relationship with choosing competitions that offer higher rewards. The study distinguishes confidence grounded in improved performance from confidence that exceeds what actual performance supports.

Research question

How does AI assistance shape people’s estimates of their relative performance, their choice of competition, and the mix of participants entering higher-reward tournaments?

Approach

The planned experiment will connect baseline ability, observed task performance, self-assessments, and choices between tournaments with different rewards. A key goal is to examine whether AI assistance changes how actual performance and perceived relative standing relate to tournament choice. The experimental tasks and procedures are currently being developed.

My contribution

As first author, I am developing the research framework and translating it into an experimental design.

Experimental design in progress. Study tasks and procedures are being developed.
